[orange]DriveDogs: Economy

[normal]A combined realism & economy overhaul, assembled section by section from hand-picked mods
and custom tuning. This description grows one section at a time as each system is added.
--------------------
[orange]Damage & Wear
[normal]Crashes hurt more, and part of that damage never fully goes away - the harder you drive,
the more it costs you over the truck's whole life. Routine wear from just driving stays gentle,
so careful drivers aren't punished for racking up the miles.

What changes:
- Collisions deal 2x damage to your truck and trailer, and more of that damage transfers
  between the two - hitting something is a bigger deal than in vanilla.
- Engine and transmission take a larger share of crash damage.
- Part of crash damage is now permanent and can't be cleared by any repair - reckless driving
  leaves a lasting mark on the truck (vanilla already worked this way; this pack raises it).
- The cabin and chassis now pick up a small amount of wear just from driving distance -
  long-haul trucks will need occasional service even without a single crash.
- Engine and transmission wear slower per km than vanilla, so that part of routine servicing
  is a bit less frequent.
- Wheel wear is unchanged from vanilla.

What does NOT change:
- Vanilla's crash-to-cargo damage behavior.
- How repairs and service work - only how much damage/wear they need to clear.
--------------------
[orange]Fines & Police
[normal]Fines are no longer "magical" - you won't get ticketed out of thin air for things no
officer or camera could have seen. But when you genuinely get caught, it costs exactly what it
costs in the base game - full price. Rule-breaking should be a real risk, not free.

What changes:
- Several violations that vanilla could fine you for with zero police or camera anywhere nearby
  (wrong way, no headlights at night, skipping a mandatory break, stopping on the hard
  shoulder, driving a damaged vehicle) now only get ticketed if a real police car is actually
  there to catch you.
- Speed cameras, weigh stations, and border checkpoints still catch you reliably - those are
  real fixed infrastructure, not "magic," so they're left alone (with a very slight realism
  tweak: cameras occasionally miss, like real ones).
- Red light running now has a chance of being camera-caught even without police nearby,
  instead of vanilla's flat rate.
- When a police car is genuinely nearby, it's now more likely to catch several of the
  violations above than vanilla was - since we removed the "free pass" of no police around,
  getting caught by an actual patrol is a bit more reliable to compensate.
- A couple of "gotcha" instant tickets are softened: speeding and illegal-trailer violations
  now need a real police car to notice you for a few extra seconds before ticketing, instead of
  an almost-instant vanilla catch.
- Repeated fines from the same lingering police car are spaced out more, so one patrol can't
  spam you with tickets every few seconds.

What does NOT change:
- Every fine amount - all exactly vanilla. Nothing here is a discount.
- The speeding fine's scaling with how far over the limit you are - still vanilla's full curve,
  up to 12.5x the base fine for extreme speeding.
- How much a nearby police car increases your fine when it does catch you - still full vanilla
  severity.

Heads up: some of the violations above (mandatory break, damaged vehicle, hard shoulder, wrong
way, no lights) now only trigger when a real police car is nearby, and ETS2's police aren't
everywhere. In practice that makes those specific violations noticeably rarer overall, not just
fairer - that's the intended tradeoff for removing "magical" detection.
--------------------
[orange]Used Truck Market
[normal]Second-hand trucks are a real bargain-vs-risk decision now, not a showroom-fresh clone of
a new truck with fewer zeros on the price tag.

What changes:
- Every used truck now needs a first service - it comes with real repairable wear (a garage
  bill), instead of vanilla's used trucks being mechanically as good as new.
- Mileage can run much higher than vanilla - up to 1,200,000 km on the highest-mileage listings,
  instead of vanilla's 100,000 km ceiling. There are no near-new low-mileage listings anymore
  (vanilla's occasional very-low-mileage find is gone).
- High-mileage trucks can carry real, lasting damage that no repair fully clears - so a cheap
  bargain can mean a genuinely compromised truck, not just a dusty one.
- Prices react much more strongly to a truck's condition - both the repairable wear and the
  permanent damage now swing the price a lot more than in vanilla.
- The market is bigger (18-36 trucks at once) and refreshes about twice a day, so there's
  usually something new to look at between jobs without being able to buy up the whole lot in
  one sitting.
- Individual listings vary more in price than vanilla, so two similar-looking trucks won't
  always cost the same.

Career progression: new players start with access to a smaller, safer slice of the used market -
lower mileage ceiling, less severe permanent damage possible, and a narrower selection of truck
models. This opens up as you level up, reaching full access to every truck model, the full
1,200,000 km ceiling, and the roughest bargain-basement listings by level 30. Level 15 is a
clear milestone along the way, well ahead of full unlock - a meaningful upgrade mid-career, not
just a token bump.

What does NOT change:
- How a used truck performs or continues to wear once you own it - that's the Damage & Wear
  system above, applied exactly the same as any other truck from the moment of purchase.
--------------------
[orange]Operating Consequences
[normal]Running a haulage business should feel like one - mistakes and emergencies cost real
money, without turning every ordinary job into a grind. Job pay itself is untouched; only what
happens when something goes wrong changes.

What changes:
- Abandoning a job, damaging cargo, and reselling a truck all cost noticeably more than vanilla -
  2x the abandoned-job fine, 2x the cargo-damage penalty (both the flat fee and the percentage of
  the job's pay it eats), and a real cut to what you get back selling a truck (40% instead of
  vanilla's 60%).
- Damaging cargo now costs experience points more too, matching the money penalty - a bad
  delivery sets back your wallet and your career progress by the same degree.
- Getting stuck: emergency towing, emergency refueling, and emergency EV charging all cost
  significantly more than vanilla. These only ever happen by choice (running dry, ending up
  somewhere a tow is needed) - avoid them entirely with normal careful driving, or pay a real
  price when you don't.
- Keeping fuel topped up at your own garage is less of a blanket discount than vanilla (5%
  instead of 15%) - still worth doing, just not free money.
- Delivery deadlines are now built around a more realistic average pace (accounting for loading,
  traffic, and rest stops) instead of near-highway-cruising speed - so there's less pressure to
  speed just to make the clock. The easy/medium/hard difficulty thresholds that decide a job's
  pay bonus are recalibrated to match, so the mix of job difficulty you see stays close to
  vanilla's rather than quietly getting easier.

What does NOT change:
- Job base pay and difficulty-tier bonus multipliers - all exactly vanilla. This section is
  entirely about the cost side, not what you earn (job pay itself is addressed in Job Types &
  Skills below).
- Driver hiring, wages, and AI driver economics - unchanged, a separate topic from your own
  operating costs.
- Bank loans - amounts and interest rates are exactly vanilla, so capital to expand is just as
  available as always even though day-to-day mistakes cost more.
--------------------
[orange]Job Types & Skills
[normal]The first piece of a bigger "meaningful jobs and cargo" pass - matching pay to who's
actually carrying the risk, and finally rewarding a skill vanilla forgot to reward.

What changes:
- Cargo Market jobs (where you own the trailer as well as the truck) now pay noticeably more per
  km than Freight Market jobs - 1.4x the base rate instead of vanilla's barely-there 1.0x, a real
  premium for carrying full responsibility, including the trailer's own damage and insurance risk
  through the Damage & Wear and Operating Consequences systems above.
- The ADR (hazardous materials) skill now grants a revenue bonus as you rank it up, exactly like
  the Valuable Cargo, Fragile Cargo, and Urgent Delivery skills already did (+5% per rank, up to
  +30% at max rank). Vanilla never actually gave this skill a pay bonus at all - specializing in
  hazmat leveled up your experience but never your paycheck. This pack fixes that gap rather than
  hand-tuning hazardous cargo's base pay directly, so it stays consistent with how every other
  cargo specialty already works.

What does NOT change:
- Quick Jobs and Freight Market pay rates - unchanged, this round only touches Cargo Market.
- Every other skill (Long Distance, Valuable Cargo, Fragile Cargo, Urgent Delivery, Eco-driving) -
  all exactly vanilla.

More to come: cargo variety (value, urgency, mass, and hazard class pay differentiation across
individual cargo types) is a bigger, separate pass and will land in its own update.
