[orange]DriveDogs: Economy

[normal]A combined realism & economy overhaul, assembled section by section from hand-picked mods
and custom tuning. This description grows one section at a time as each system is added.
--------------------
[orange]Damage & Wear
[normal]Crashes hurt more, and part of that damage never fully goes away - the harder you drive,
the more it costs you over the truck's whole life. Routine wear from just driving stays gentle,
so careful drivers aren't punished for racking up the miles.

What changes:
- Collisions deal 2x damage to your truck and trailer, and more of that damage transfers
  between the two - hitting something is a bigger deal than in vanilla.
- Engine and transmission take a larger share of crash damage.
- Part of crash damage is now permanent and can't be cleared by any repair - reckless driving
  leaves a lasting mark on the truck (vanilla already worked this way; this pack raises it).
- The cabin and chassis now pick up a small amount of wear just from driving distance -
  long-haul trucks will need occasional service even without a single crash.
- Engine and transmission wear slower per km than vanilla, so that part of routine servicing
  is a bit less frequent.
- Wheel wear is unchanged from vanilla.

What does NOT change:
- Vanilla's crash-to-cargo damage behavior.
- How repairs and service work - only how much damage/wear they need to clear.
--------------------
[orange]Fines & Police
[normal]Fines are no longer "magical" - you won't get ticketed out of thin air for things no
officer or camera could have seen. But when you genuinely get caught, it costs exactly what it
costs in the base game - full price. Rule-breaking should be a real risk, not free.

What changes:
- Several violations that vanilla could fine you for with zero police or camera anywhere nearby
  (wrong way, no headlights at night, skipping a mandatory break, stopping on the hard
  shoulder, driving a damaged vehicle) now only get ticketed if a real police car is actually
  there to catch you.
- Speed cameras, weigh stations, and border checkpoints still catch you reliably - those are
  real fixed infrastructure, not "magic," so they're left alone (with a very slight realism
  tweak: cameras occasionally miss, like real ones).
- Red light running now has a chance of being camera-caught even without police nearby,
  instead of vanilla's flat rate.
- When a police car is genuinely nearby, it's now more likely to catch several of the
  violations above than vanilla was - since we removed the "free pass" of no police around,
  getting caught by an actual patrol is a bit more reliable to compensate.
- A couple of "gotcha" instant tickets are softened: speeding and illegal-trailer violations
  now need a real police car to notice you for a few extra seconds before ticketing, instead of
  an almost-instant vanilla catch.
- Repeated fines from the same lingering police car are spaced out more, so one patrol can't
  spam you with tickets every few seconds.

What does NOT change:
- Every fine amount - all exactly vanilla. Nothing here is a discount.
- The speeding fine's scaling with how far over the limit you are - still vanilla's full curve,
  up to 12.5x the base fine for extreme speeding.
- How much a nearby police car increases your fine when it does catch you - still full vanilla
  severity.

Heads up: some of the violations above (mandatory break, damaged vehicle, hard shoulder, wrong
way, no lights) now only trigger when a real police car is nearby, and ETS2's police aren't
everywhere. In practice that makes those specific violations noticeably rarer overall, not just
fairer - that's the intended tradeoff for removing "magical" detection.
